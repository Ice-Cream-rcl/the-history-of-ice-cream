The Sweet Origin of Ice Cream

A journey through time to discover how our favorite frozen treat was born.

Ancient Beginnings

The origins of frozen desserts date back thousands of years. As early as 200 BC, people in China created a dish made of rice and milk that was packed into snow to freeze. Roman Emperor Nero (A.D. 37–68) is also said to have ordered runners to fetch snow from the mountains, which was then flavored with fruits and juices.

The Persian Innovation

By 400 BC, the Persians had invented a special chilled food made of rosewater and vermicelli, which was served to royalty during the summer. They mastered the art of storing ice inside giant underground structures known as Yakhchals, keeping desserts frozen even in the middle of the desert.

Evolution in Europe

In the 13th century, Marco Polo returned to Italy from his travels in China with a recipe that closely resembled what we now know as sherbet. From Italy, the concept spread to France when Catherine de' Medici married King Henry II in 1553, introducing flavored ice treats to the French royal court.

The Modern Era

Ice cream became available to the general public in the late 17th century. The famous Café Procope in Paris introduced a recipe blending milk, cream, butter, and eggs. By the 19th century, technological advancements like mechanical refrigeration made mass production possible, giving rise to the global ice cream culture we enjoy today.
